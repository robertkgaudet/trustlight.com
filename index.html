What "verified" actually means

We measure two different things, and we keep them separate on purpose.

Safety verification

Pass or fail. Every business must clear all nine. This is what protects you from fraud.

1
Owner background check

Fraud, theft, or property-crime convictions disqualify the business.

2
License verification

Confirmed with the Louisiana State Licensing Board — not from a document they hand us.

3
Insurance confirmation

We call the insurer. General liability and workers' comp, both current.

4
Business filing

Active and in good standing with the Secretary of State.

5
Physical address

Real, verified, matches state registration. No P.O. boxes.

6
Years in business

Confirmed through state records, not self-reported.

7
Contact verification

Phone and email confirmed active and answered.

8
Review authenticity

Reviews checked for gating, manipulation, and suspicious patterns.

9
Annual re-verification

All checks repeat yearly. Failing suspends the listing.

Digital Trust Index

A 0–100 score across five areas. This tells you how findable and responsive a business is — it is not a safety rating.

Findability

Google Business Profile, consistent name and phone across the web, working local search presence.

Answerability

Whether AI assistants and search engines can actually find and describe this business correctly.

Responsiveness

We test every contact method. How long until a real person answers — regardless of what tools they use to do it.

Completeness

Services, hours, real photos, licensing stated publicly, contact info that works.

Compliance

Privacy policy, terms, accessibility, honest advertising, and disclosure when you're talking to AI.

A high Digital Trust Index does not mean a business is honest, and a low one does not mean it isn't. Plenty of excellent contractors have outdated websites. Use the safety verification to decide who is safe to hire — use this score to know how easily you'll be able to reach them. We do not score businesses on whether they buy services from us.
